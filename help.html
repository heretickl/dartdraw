Quick start: run your first event in about 10 minutes

#

Seven steps for a first event: 24 players, singles, 4 dartboards, groups then a knockout.

The example: 24 players, one evening, 4 dartboards. Everyone plays in groups of 4, then the top 2 of each group play a knockout. Work down the steps in the left-hand menu. Each one opens when the one before is saved. New words are explained in Words you will see.

  1. Create the event. Click + New tournament. Type a Tournament name and an Event name, and leave Competition type on Singles. Set the Tournament date and Number of boards available (4). Click Save event creation →. You should see: step 2, Format & branding. More: Create the event.
  2. Choose the format. Click Format to open it, choose Round robin → Knockout and set Best of legs to 3. Leave Maximum entries per group at 4 and Entries per group (advance to knockout) at 2. Under Scheduling, check it says 4 boards (new events start with 4; Edit scheduling settings changes it). Then click Save format & branding →. You should see: step 3, Chart details, with an Add entries box at the top. More: Choose a format.
  3. Add the players and draw the groups. In Add entries, type each player’s name. Pick them from the list if they have played before, or click Add “name” as a new player. When all 24 are in, click Random draw, then Review print pack →. More: Adding entries. You should see: six groups, A to F, of 4 players each, each with a board. Groups that share a board play one after another. More: Drawing the groups.
  4. Confirm, publish, print. On step 4, click Confirm all 6 charts. If spectators will follow on their phones, click Publish results now in the note above the print button first, so the QR codes print. Then click Print bracket pack and choose a printer or Save as PDF. You should see: your browser’s print window with the running order and one chart for each group. More: Printing the blank pack and QR codes.
  5. Play the groups. On step 5, Manage Tournament, click Start all games and confirm in the box. Then click the box where two players meet, type the legs each won (for example 2 and 1) and click Save score. You should see: the standings under that chart update at once. More: Entering scores.
  6. Run the knockout. When every group is finished, click Generate knockout bracket in the Knockout stage card. Enter each knockout match the same way: click it, type the legs and click Save score. You should see: the top 2 of each group, 12 players, in the bracket. Four of them start in round 2 (a bye). Each winner moves on by itself. More: Drawing the knockout from a round robin.
  7. Finish. When the final is in, go to the bottom of step 5 and click Accept & finalize. Then click Accept & finalize again in the box “Accept results as final?”. You cannot undo this; only an Admin can reopen the event. You should see: “Results finalized”, and the public page says Final results. Step 7, Print Final Chart, prints the results. More: Finish the event.

Try it first with a sample event

While you have no saved events, the welcome screen shows a Get started card with these seven steps. Its Try a sample event button opens a practice event: 12 players, round robin then knockout, with the names already typed. You can change anything in it. A banner says “This is a sample event. Nothing is saved.”

  • Save as my event keeps it: from then on it is an ordinary event in Saved tournaments.
  • Close sample throws it away. So does opening another event, starting a new one or logging out.

A sample event cannot be published, finalized or opened in a full-page window until you save it as your event.

No account yet? Signing in and passwords

#

How to get an account, sign in, and change or reset your password.

No account yet?

Accounts are created by the site admin, usually the person who runs DartDraw for your club or league. Unless the sign-in box shows a Sign up link, you cannot make one yourself: ask your admin to create it. You then get a link to choose your password, and you sign in as below.

The sign-in page and the account emails are in English and Bahasa Malaysia: see English or Bahasa Malaysia.

The sign-in box says the same under Sign in: “No account yet? Accounts are created by the site admin.” Your admin can add a line under it saying who to ask and how. If your club has no admin yet, the person who set up DartDraw for you can create one. See also Who is the Admin, and how do I reach them?

Signing in

Your site admin creates your account and sends you a link to choose a password. After that:

  1. Click Log in at the top of any public page.
  2. Type your email and password, then click Sign in.
  3. You land in the organiser app, where all your events live. New to it? Start with the Quick start.

Forgot your password? The surest way is to ask your admin for a reset link. You can also click Forgot password? under the sign-in form and type your email. If a link arrives, follow it. If nothing arrives, ask your admin.

To change your password while you are signed in, click Change password next to Log out in the left-hand menu. On a phone or a narrow window, tap the step name in the bar at the top, then Change password. Type your Current password, then your New password twice. It must be at least 8 characters. Changing it signs you out on your other devices, but not on the one you are using.

How DartDraw works: the seven steps and saving

#

The seven steps in the left-hand menu, Basic or Advanced setup, and what the save messages mean.

The seven steps

The left-hand menu lists seven steps. Work down the list. You cannot skip ahead: step 2 opens once step 1 is saved, and step 3 opens once step 2 is saved. Each step’s title has a small ? beside it that opens the matching part of this Help.

  1. Event creation: name the event, choose Singles, Doubles or Teams, and set the date and boards.
  2. Format & branding: choose the format, legs, boards, logos and rules.
  3. Chart details: names, seeds, organisations and the draw.
  4. Confirm & Print Bracket: check each chart, confirm it and print the blank pack.
  5. Manage Tournament: start the games, enter scores, share live results and finish the event with Accept & finalize at the bottom.
  6. Finalize Standings: a read-only look at the final standings. It does not close the event.
  7. Print Final Chart: print the pack with every result filled in.

Basic or Advanced setup

At the top of step 1, under Setup mode, you choose “Basic” or “Advanced”. In this guide they are called Basic setup and Advanced setup.

  • “Basic” is the essentials: format, groups and scores. New events start here.
  • “Advanced” adds scheduling details (level of play, start time and breaks) and different best-of-legs for each knockout round. It also adds the Plate pool, sponsor logos with a footer line, and your own tie-break order.

You can switch at any time, on step 1 or with the Switch to Advanced link at the top of step 2. Switching never deletes anything you have already set.

Saving your work

On steps 1 and 2, what you type is saved when you click the button at the bottom of the form: Save event creation → or Save format & branding →. After that, every change is saved to your account by itself, a moment after you make it. Names save when you click or tab out of the box.

The save status sits in the left-hand menu, under your name. On a narrow screen it is in the bar at the top, and in the full-page window it is in the header. It can say:

Saving…
Your change is on its way to your account.
Saved
All good. Your change is stored, and any device you sign in on will see it.
Couldn’t save — click to retry
The change did not reach the server, usually because the connection dropped. Check your internet, then click the message to try again. Keep the page open until it says Saved. Until then DartDraw will not open another event or start a new one: it keeps you on this one and says “Last change not saved yet”. You can choose Leave without saving, but that change is then gone. Log out waits for it the same way, and if you close or reload the page meanwhile, your browser asks you first.
Updated elsewhere — click to reload
A newer copy of this event was saved in another tab, window or device, so the copy on your screen is out of date. A red banner also appears at the top of the page: “Updated elsewhere — reload to get the latest.” Your last change in this window was not saved, and from now on this window saves nothing for this event, so it cannot write over the newer results. Click Reload on the banner (or click the message) to load the latest copy. Then check it and enter your last change again.
Deleted elsewhere — this event is no longer saved
The event was deleted (or moved to another organiser) in another window or device. Nothing you change here can be saved. Open another event.
Finalizing…
You accepted the results and DartDraw is closing the event. Wait for it to finish.
This event is finalized — only an Admin can change it
The event is closed. See Finish the event.

A sample event is never saved, so it shows no save status. See Try it first with a sample event.

English or Bahasa Malaysia: screens, printed sheets and emails

#

The EN | BM switch, the language each event prints in, and why account emails come in both languages.

The EN | BM switch

DartDraw is in English and in Bahasa Malaysia. In the organiser app the EN | BM switch is at the top right, next to Help. On a narrow screen it is in the bar at the top, or in the menu that opens from the step name, under “Bahasa:”. The public pages and the sign-in page have the same switch at the top (the sign-in box that opens over a public page follows that page).

A click changes every word on the screen at once. Switching saves nothing and changes nothing in your event: you stay on the same step, an open box stays open, and what you typed stays in its box.

DartDraw remembers your choice in this browser. The organiser app, the public pages (this Help too) and the sign-in page share it: choose BM on one and the others open in BM as well. The Help link and the ? links open this Help in the language on your screen. The public results site, where spectators follow your event, has its own EN | BM switch and remembers its own choice: it does not change the language of your screens, and your choice does not change theirs.

Printed sheets language

The language of the paper is a setting of each event, separate from the language on your screen. Printed sheets language sits with the other print settings on step 4, Confirm & Print Bracket, and on step 7, Print Final Chart: choose English or Bahasa Malaysia.

  • Every sheet of the event uses it: the charts and brackets, the running order, the rules sheet, the lineup sheets, the Players A–Z and Boards sheets, and the print previews on steps 4 and 7.
  • It belongs to the event, so the paper is the same whoever prints it and whatever language their screen shows. For example, your screens can be in BM while the sheets print in English.
  • A new event starts with the language your screen shows when you create it. Events made before this setting existed print in English. A duplicated event keeps the setting.
  • Names, and everything you typed (the event name, the rules, the sponsor footer), print as you typed them. Automatic names such as “Player A1” are stored in the language of the screen when they were made.
  • On a finalized event the setting is locked, like the other print settings.

Emails in both languages

DartDraw does not know which language you read when it emails you, so every account email is in both languages: English first, then Bahasa Malaysia, in the same message, with both in the subject line. That includes the email to verify your email address, the one with a link to reset your password, and the one you get when an admin creates your account or sends you a new password link. The link is the same in both halves.

Set up an event

Create the event (step 1)

#

Name it, choose Singles, Doubles or Teams, and set the date and the number of boards. Entries are added on step 3.

Click + New tournament at the top of the left-hand menu. Step 1 is called Event creation. Fill in:

  1. Setup mode: “Basic” or “Advanced”. See Basic or Advanced setup.
  2. Tournament name: the day or competition, for example Spring Open 2026. It is printed on every sheet.
  3. Event name: this competition inside it, for example Open Singles.
  4. Event category: Open, Women or Youth. It is printed on the charts.
  5. Competition type: Singles, Doubles or Teams.
  6. Tournament date: shown on the charts. The public home page also uses it to list upcoming events.
  7. Number of boards available: how many dartboards you have. You can change it later on step 2, under Scheduling.

There is no number of entries to type. You add the entries (players, pairs or teams) one by one on step 3, from your players list or as new names.

Click Save event creation → to move on to step 2.

Singles, Doubles or Teams

In Singles, one entry is one player.

In Doubles, one entry is a pair of two players. On step 3, click Add pair and choose the two players. A team name is optional: with one the pair shows as “TEAM NAME : Ahmad / Hakim”, without one as “Ahmad / Hakim”. Charts, results and the public page show that name.

In Teams, one entry is a team. On step 3, click Add team, type the team name and add its players (as many as you like). The team shows by its name everywhere. Captains still write their line-up on the day. A team match is made of segments that you set on step 2. See Set up a Teams event.

Boards and the number of entries

The number of entries is however many you add on step 3. You can add and remove entries there until the draw. After the draw, take someone out with Withdraw or Mark no-show instead. See Why can’t I change the entries after the games start?

If you see a message that your account allows up to a certain number of entries per event, contact your admin.

Your saved events

Your events are listed under Saved tournaments in the left-hand menu. Click one to open it. Each has three small buttons:

  • Rename changes the tournament name.
  • Duplicate makes a copy called “… (copy)” with everything in it. The copy is not published, not listed and not finalized, so you can edit it.
  • Delete removes the event from your account for good. It cannot be undone.

On step 1, Discard / New tournament throws away the form and starts a fresh event. A finalized event cannot be renamed or deleted by you.

Choose a format (step 2)

#

Which format suits your field, then round robin, knockout (one bracket, or Group Boards then finals) or round robin then knockout, plus legs, boards, branding and rules.

Step 2 is Format & branding. It has four sections: Format, Scheduling, Branding & print and Rules. Click a section heading to open or close it. Click Save format & branding → when you are done.

Which format should I choose?

A small field, and time for everyone to play everyone in their group
Round robin. Everyone gets the same number of matches, and the tables decide the places.
A big field and little time
Knockout, Single bracket. It has the fewest matches: lose once and you are out.
One evening, everyone plays a few matches, then a final
Round robin → Knockout. Groups first, then the top of each group play a knockout. The Quick start uses it for 24 players.
A very big field, with many boards
Knockout, then Group Boards, then finals. Each group of players has its own board and its own little bracket, and the group champions meet in the Finals.
Teams of players
Choose Teams as the Competition type on step 1, then any of the formats above. See Set up a Teams event.

Not sure how long it takes? With a start time set (Advanced setup), DartDraw estimates it for you. See Scheduling.

The formats

Under Competition format pick one of three:

  • Round robin: entries play within their groups. Everyone in a group plays everyone else in it once.
  • Knockout: direct elimination. Lose and you are out. Under Knockout type choose one of two:
    • Single bracket: the usual choice. It is one bracket. If the number of entries does not fill it, the app adds byes in round 1 and gives them to the best seeds.
    • Group Boards, then finals: small knockout groups first, each on its own board, so every group has a champion before the finals. See below.
  • Round robin → Knockout: a group stage first, then the top finishers go into a bracket.

Best of legs

The Best of legs box sets how long a match is. A leg is one game of darts. Odd numbers are best. Best of 5 means the first to 3 legs wins. When you enter a result, the winner must have exactly that many legs and the loser fewer. An even number shows a warning, because it can end level.

A Teams event has no Best of legs box: each segment of a team match has its own. See Set up a Teams event.

Round robin

Choose Maximum entries per group (3 to 6). A blue box under it shows the split DartDraw recommends, for example how many groups and how many matches each group plays. DartDraw balances the groups when it draws them.

Round robin → Knockout

  • Entries per group (advance to knockout): how many from each group go into the bracket.
  • How entries are placed into the bracket: Seeded by group standing (recommended) puts group winners ahead of runners-up. It also keeps entries from the same group apart for as long as possible. Random draw mixes them up.
  • Plate pool (Advanced setup): tick Also run a Plate pool knockout bracket for entries that don’t advance. Leave Plate entries per group empty to send everyone who does not advance to the Plate pool. Type a number to cap it, so anyone ranked below that is out.

The bracket is not drawn until every group has finished. You draw it in step 5. See Run the knockout.

Group Boards, then finals (a Knockout option)

Choose Knockout, then Group Boards, then finals. It suits big fields and anyone who wants a group champion before the finals. Instead of one big bracket you choose a number of small groups, and each group is a little knockout on its own board. Every group ends with a champion, and the champions meet in a Finals bracket. You do not have to use it, and it is not offered with the round robin formats.

  • Number of groups: at least 2, at most 128. Each group needs at least 2 players and holds at most 128 (a group is one knockout bracket). DartDraw tells you if the numbers do not fit. Moving a player or placing a late entry into a group that already has 128 players is refused; swap two players instead.
  • Best of legs inside each group can differ from the Finals. The Finals uses Best of legs (or your knockout leg settings in Advanced setup).
  • In step 3 you click Draw groups. Seeded players are spread one per group, and the groups can be changed by hand until you confirm them.
  • The Finals chart is built as soon as the last group is confirmed, with slots such as “Winner of Group 3”.

More legs in later rounds

In Advanced setup a knockout has a tick box that raises the legs in later rounds. (A Teams event does not have it: every round uses the same segments.) Tick it and you can set Early rounds, Quarterfinal, Semifinal and Final separately. Every round before the quarterfinal uses Early rounds. The Plate pool has its own, independent settings.

Scheduling

Click Edit scheduling settings. Number of boards available sets the board choices later on. In Advanced setup you can also set Level of play (Professional, Amateur or Novice), an Anticipated start time and breaks. DartDraw uses these to estimate how long the event takes and to show “On schedule” or “Running behind” while you play. These are rough guides. They are never printed.

Branding and rules

  • Tournament logo: best as a square or near-square PNG. DartDraw resizes it for you.
  • Sponsor logos and Optional print rules / footer text (Advanced setup): sponsor logos print in the chart footer. Pick a rectangle or a square for each one.
  • Walkover, conduct & other rules: free text printed on the rules sheet.
  • Tie-break order (Advanced setup): move the rules up or down. See How do tie-breaks work?

Add entries, seeds and organisations (step 3)

#

Naming entries (players, pairs or teams), organisations and seeding.

Looking for check-in, withdrawals or no-shows? See Check people in and start the games and No-shows and players who leave.

Adding entries

Step 3 starts with an Add entries box. In Singles, type a name or club: players from your list appear as you type, so click one to add them. For someone new, click Add “name” as a new player (it is also saved to your players list, with the club if you type one). Or click Add to this event only, for example for a nickname you do not want to keep.

In Doubles, click Add pair; in Teams, click Add team (or Copy a team from a past event). Each player can be in only one entry per event: DartDraw says, for example, “Sam is already in THE POINTERS57”.

Each entry has Remove until the draw. You can still correct a Singles name by typing over it. Events made before this way of adding entries keep their entries as they are.

Your players list

Click Players in the left-hand menu to see everyone you have added: name, club, how many of your events they played and when they last played. There you can add, edit and remove players, merge two entries for the same person (tick both, then Merge), and Add players from a past event. Changing your list never changes a past event or a printed chart. Your list is private to you.

Organisations

Use the Organisation (optional) box for a club or organisation name. Then tick the box that keeps them apart:

  • Round robin: the Organisation (optional) box is always there on step 3. Tick Avoid same-organisation clashes. It also reshuffles existing clashes where it can. In Advanced setup the same option is under Rules on step 2.
  • Knockout and Group Boards: the Organisation (optional) box is hidden at first. It appears once you tick Track organisation (avoid same-organisation matchups in the first round) on step 3.

It is a best effort. If there is no safe swap anywhere, a clash is left in place.

In a knockout and in Group Boards groups, entries whose organisation is filled in are kept apart as long as possible, whether or not one of the tick boxes is on. The knockout after a round robin (and its Plate pool) does not use organisations: it is placed by group and finishing position only, so the bracket printed before the round robin is exactly the real draw.

Each draw is given about a second of work at most. In a big bracket (from about 30 entries) with many entries from one or two organisations, the result is the best DartDraw found in that time. Entries are still spread out, but a little less evenly than a much longer search could manage. Now and then a pair meets a round earlier than it might.

Seeds

In a Knockout, step 3 shows Participants & Seeding. Seeding is off by default, which means a fresh random draw every time you generate the bracket. Tick Seed this bracket, then use the up and down arrows to put entries in order. Number of seeds (4, 8, 16, 32 or 64) sets how many of the top entries show a seed number on the printed chart. Everyone is still placed using your order. Seeds 1 and 2 can only meet in the final, and the best seeds get any byes.

In Round robin → Knockout, seeds come from the group results. Entries show labels like “A1” (group A, first place). In Group Boards, the same seed list is used and seeds are spread one per group.

Draw the groups or the bracket (step 3)

#

Random draw, swaps, boards for each group, and where a knockout or Group Boards draw happens.

Drawing the groups

In a round robin, step 3 is called Draw & groups. Entries stay as “Not drawn” until you click Random draw. Click it again to deal everyone out afresh; DartDraw asks first because it discards your manual swaps. Once any group has started, Random draw is greyed out and Swap with… is no longer offered for entries in a started group. Moving entries then would leave results with people who did not play them.

  • Switch between Flat list and Grouped view. The flat list has a search box for name or organisation.
  • Swap with… opens a list of groups and positions. Pick one. If it is occupied the two entries swap. If it is blank the entry moves there.
  • Each group has a board. In the Grouped view you tick Board(s). Groups on the same board play one after another. Tick more than one board to share a group’s matches across them.

The first match on every chart is 1 v 2. Each chart also lists the match order and suggests a marker for every match, chosen from the other entries in the group. A Teams event has no markers: each team scores its own match on the lineup sheet.

Drawing a knockout bracket

A Knockout has no groups to draw. Set the seeds on step 3 if you want them (see Seeds), then click Generate bracket on step 4. Byes go to the best seeds; see How are byes decided in a knockout?

In Group Boards, click Draw groups on step 3. See Group Boards, then finals.

Check and print the blank pack (step 4)

#

Confirm the charts, then print the blank pack: charts, rules sheet, logos and QR codes.

Confirming

Step 4 is Confirm & Print Bracket. Check each group, then click Confirm this chart, or Confirm all N charts. The confirm box warns you about problems such as a missing entry or two identical names. If you later change who is in a confirmed group, it is un-confirmed for you with the note “Un-confirmed automatically”. Confirm it again.

For a Knockout, step 4 has Generate bracket (or Regenerate bracket). Regenerating after results exist wipes them, and asks first.

Printing the blank pack

Click Print bracket pack. The pack is A4 landscape and can include, in this order:

  • the rules & format sheet, always the first page when it is ticked (tick box on the same screen);
  • a master running order, straight after the rules sheet (or first, if you leave the rules sheet out). It has one column per board (up to 8 on a sheet) and lists each board’s groups in playing order, with a tick box for each match. A board with more groups than fit continues on the next sheet. A group is never split;
  • one chart for each confirmed group;
  • the knockout bracket, or a projected bracket with labels like A1 before the groups are played;
  • in Group Boards, a Players A–Z sheet, a Boards sheet, one sheet for each group and the Finals;
  • in a Teams event, a lineup sheet for each fixture if you tick the box for it. See The lineup sheet.

Your browser’s print window opens. Choose a printer, or choose Save as PDF to make a file. Print the blank pack before you start games; DartDraw suggests it when you start.

Want QR codes on the sheets? Publish before you print. While publishing is off, step 4 shows a note above the print button: “Publishing is off, so these sheets will have no QR code”. Click Publish results now in it (the same as ticking Publish results on step 5), then print. Sheets printed earlier have no QR code; print them again after publishing to get them.

Knockout bracket sheets and paper size

Every knockout bracket prints as one two-sided chart on one sheet: the main bracket, the Plate pool and the projected bracket. The left half and the right half run towards the Final in the middle, with the title above it and the Champion box below. An entry with a first-round bye starts in round 2 with a small BYE tag. Names up to 24 characters print in full. A longer name is cut with a small number, and the full name is printed at the foot of the sheet. Group Boards keeps its own chart.

Pick the Knockout bracket paper size (A4, A3, A2 or A1) on step 4 or step 7. Under it, a fit check shows how big the names print on each size. Names are readable from 7 pt. When they would print between 7 and 8 pt, a note suggests the size that gives 8 pt or more, with a button to switch the event to it. It never stops you printing.

When a bracket is too big to print readably on the size you chose, printing first asks what to do:

  • Use A3 (recommended) (or whichever size it needs): the event’s saved paper size changes to that size and the bracket prints on one sheet of it. Set the printer to the same size.
  • Split over N A4 sheets, where the bracket can be split. The outer rounds of each half print as sections on A4 sheets: L1, L2 … for the left half and R1, R2 … for the right, numbered top to bottom. A Finals sheet holds the last rounds. All of them use the same readable scale. Each section shows where its winners go (“to Finals 5-1”). Each first box on the Finals sheet shows where its entry comes from: “L1 · 4-1” is the winner of match 4-1 on section L1. Small + marks show where winners leave a section and enter the Finals sheet. On an ordinary office printer, this is usually the one to choose.
  • Keep A4 (your size): the whole chart is shrunk to fit one sheet, keeping its shape. The names may be too small to read when printed, so a note on the print screen says so and offers the recommended size. The note is never printed.

If no paper size up to A1 holds the bracket at 7 pt, the box says that even A1 needs to shrink it. It then offers only the split and the shrink.

Your answer is remembered for the event. You are asked again when it no longer fits: when you pick a different paper size, or after another draw that needs a different size.

Logos, sponsors and the rules sheet

Your tournament logo goes in every sheet header. Sponsor logos (Advanced setup) print in the footer when Include sponsor footer on printed charts is ticked. The rules sheet is written for you from your settings: the format, scoring, how many go through and the tie-break order. It adds anything you typed in Walkover, conduct & other rules.

QR codes

Enter and correct scores (step 5)

#

Enter scores, correct or clear a result, read the standings, adjust positions and keep a private incident log.

Step 5 is Manage Tournament. This is where you spend match day. To start the games, see Start all games.

Entering scores

Round robin: click the box where two entries meet. Type each side’s legs and click Save score. The winner must have exactly the legs needed for your best-of, and the loser fewer. Positions, points and leg totals update at once in the standings under each chart.

Knockout: click a match, type the legs and click Save score. There are no draws. The winner moves to the next round.

Teams: type the total legs each team won across the whole match. See Entering a team result.

Correcting or clearing a result

  • To correct a score, click it again, type the right legs and click Save score.
  • To clear a result, for example a score typed on the wrong match, click it and choose Clear result. DartDraw asks “Clear this result?” first: click Clear result again, or Cancel. The match goes back to not played, and the round robin standings are worked out again. A walkover can be cleared the same way. A match marked Neither showed has Reverse it instead.
  • In a knockout, if you change who won a match, or clear it, every later match that the old winner had reached is cleared as well, right up to the final, and so is the champion. You then enter those results again. If any of those later matches already has a result, DartDraw asks you first and lists them. For example: “Changing this result will clear 2 later results, and you will need to enter them again”, plus the champion if there is one. Click Change result to go ahead, or Cancel to leave everything as it was. Correcting only the score, with the same winner, changes nothing later and asks nothing. This works the same in the Plate pool, a Teams knockout, the Group Boards Finals and the full-page window. Inside a Group Boards group, a new winner clears the later matches of that group in the same way without asking. It is refused if a Finals match that depends on it already has a score.
  • After the knockout has been drawn from a round robin, a group result can still be corrected with Save score, but it can no longer be cleared. The group would be unfinished, and the knockout is only drawn from finished groups. If a correction changes who goes through, use Regenerate from current standings (it discards any knockout results, and asks first).
  • A finalized event cannot be changed. See Changing a finalized event.

Standings

A ? means they are still level. The small ? link beside the word Standings opens the table words. See also How do tie-breaks work?

Adjusting positions

If a round robin tie needs your decision, click Adjust positions… on the group in Manage Tournament. Type the position you want for each entry and click Save. Manual positions get an asterisk (*) on the chart. Reset all brings back the automatic order.

Incident log

No-shows and players who leave

#

What to do when an entry does not turn up or has to stop: before their group starts, during the groups, during the knockout, and after the event is finalized.

Whatever stage you are at, results already played are never removed. What you click depends on how far the event has gone.

Before the draw, or before their group starts

  1. Go to step 3, Chart details.
  2. Click Withdraw next to the entry.
  3. Before the draw they are simply removed. After the draw, type a replacement name to fill the slot, or leave the box blank for a bye, then click Withdraw.
  4. If their group was confirmed, it is un-confirmed. Check it on step 4 and click Confirm this chart again.

A Knockout or Group Boards event has no Withdraw button on step 3. Once the bracket is drawn, use the knockout steps below.

During the round robin

Do not use Withdraw: once the group has started it is refused, because it would remove results already played. Instead:

  1. On step 5, Manage Tournament, click Mark no-show… on their group’s chart.
  2. Choose the entry.

Every match they have not played in that group becomes a walkover loss, tagged WO, so their opponents get the points. You can also start from step 3: click Mark no-show… beside their name, then Mark no-show instead. To take it back, use the undo link in the no-show box. That link is there only until the knockout is drawn from the groups; after that, enter the real score over a WO box instead.

During the knockout

  1. Find their next match in the bracket on step 5 (or in the full-page window).
  2. Click Mark no-show… under the match and choose them.

Their opponent goes through by a walkover, tagged WO. If their next match has no opponent yet, wait until it has one, then do the same. After a round robin, Mark no-show… beside their name on step 3 does this for you when the match is ready. The group results stay as they are and the knockout is not drawn again. In Group Boards it works the same way inside each group and in the Finals.

After the event is finalized

Nothing can be changed any more. Only an Admin can reopen the event. See Changing a finalized event.

No-shows and walkovers

In a round robin, once the games have started, click Mark no-show… on the group’s chart and choose the entry. Every match they have not yet played in that group is filled in as a walkover loss and tagged WO. A walkover is a win by the majority of legs to nil, and it counts as a played match. Real results you already entered are never overwritten. Until the knockout is drawn from the groups, the same box has an undo link. After that a no-show can no longer be undone (it would leave the group unfinished). Entering a real score over a WO box still replaces it.

In a knockout or Group Boards match, click Mark no-show… under the match and choose who did not show. The other side wins by a walkover and goes through, tagged WO.

Neither showed

This is for knockout and Group Boards matches only; a round robin has no such choice. If neither side turns up, choose Neither showed in the same box. Nobody advances and the next opponent gets a bye. If the match is a group’s final in Group Boards, that group finishes with no winner. Its Finals slot reads “Group N: no winner”, which becomes a bye. To undo it, open the match and click Reverse it, or just enter a score. On the public page the match reads “Neither attended”, or NO-SHOW in the bracket Chart view.

Withdraw: when it works and when it is refused

In a round robin, click Withdraw next to the entry on step 3. You can do this until their group starts. Before the draw it simply removes them. After the draw you can type a replacement name to fill the slot, or leave it blank for a bye. A group whose entries changed is un-confirmed so you can check it again.

Once their group has started (after Start all games), once they have a result, or once a drawn bracket holds them, Withdraw is no longer possible. It would remove results that are already in and change the table. On step 3 the Withdraw button is then replaced by Mark no-show…. Once they are marked it shows a No-show tag instead, and nothing when they have no match left to play. Click it and DartDraw explains why and offers Mark no-show instead. In a group, their remaining matches become walkover losses. In the knockout, they lose their next match by a walkover and the opponent goes through. Results already played stay.

The Knockout list on step 3 has no Withdraw button. If someone is not coming, use Mark no-show… on their match once the bracket is drawn.

Full-page window: keeping windows in step, and its messages

#

Windows do not update each other while you look at them. Score in one window at a time, and know what the window says when something is missing.

Keeping the windows in step

This matters, so read it once.

  • Each window loads your saved event when it opens, and saves what you do in it to your account.
  • The windows do not update each other while you look at them. A score you enter in one does not appear in the other straight away.
  • When you switch to a window (click into it, or bring it back on screen), it checks for a newer copy of the event. If there is one, it loads it, stays on the same step and shows “Updated with the latest scores”. It skips this while a change of yours is still being saved, while a box is open, while you are typing in a box or have typed something not yet saved, and on steps 1 to 3. So check the scores before you enter the next one. Windows in the same browser tell each other when they save, so the window you switch to picks up the other one’s score straight away. Otherwise (for example a change made on another device) it checks at most every few seconds.
  • If you score in a window that is out of date, that score is not saved. DartDraw refuses it so it cannot write over the newer results. The red banner “Updated elsewhere — reload to get the latest” appears in that window (the full-page window too). Until you reload, that window saves nothing.
  • When you see the banner, click Reload before you enter anything else. Then check the scores and enter your last one again.
  • So run one window per event at a time for entering scores, and use the other only to look at. Keep the full-page window on one device only. Do not open the same event on a second device or in a second tab and score there as well.
  • If you are not sure a window is up to date, press F5 in it before you score. In the main window you can also click the event in Saved tournaments.
  • The full-page window shows its save status in the header. After you enter a score there, check that it says Saved. If it says “Couldn’t save — click to retry”, check your connection and click it.

The simplest habit: score in one window, and use the other for showing.

When it says something is missing

  • “No bracket yet — generate one in Confirm & Print Bracket, then reopen this window.” Generate the bracket in the main window, then open the window again or reload it.
  • “No confirmed charts yet — confirm at least one group in Confirm & Print Bracket, then reopen this window.” The round robin window needs at least one confirmed group.
  • “No tournament open.” There is no event to show. Close the window and open it again from Manage Tournament.

Finalized events

A finalized event opens view-only. Score boxes and matches do nothing. Sometimes a window that did not know the event was finalized tries to save a change. A notice then appears at the top: “This event is finalized. Your change was not saved; only an Admin can change it.”

Teams events

Set up a Teams event

#

Set up a team match as segments, and choose where a tie-breaker is used.

In a Teams event every match between two teams is made of segments, played one after another. For example: a foursome, then singles and doubles games. You set the segments once, on step 2. Every match in the event uses them: in the groups, every knockout round, the Plate pool and Group Boards alike. A team is just its name. DartDraw keeps no list of players. To enter results and print lineup sheets, see How a team match is scored.

Setting up the segments

Open the Format section of step 2. Under Team match segments (played in order) each row is one segment, with:

  • a type: Singles, Doubles, Trios or Foursome;
  • a starting score: 301, 501, 701 or 1001;
  • a best of number of legs. It must be odd (1, 3, 5…). An even number shows a warning, and Save format & branding → refuses it, because it could end level. The same goes for the tie-breaker when it is used.

Click + Add segment for another row. Use the arrows to move a segment up or down, and ✕ to remove one. There is always at least one, and at most 8, so the lineup sheet fits on one page. The editor is there in Basic and Advanced setup. A Teams event has no Best of legs box and no extra legs for later rounds, because each segment sets its own.

Like every other setting on step 2, your changes count only when you click Save format & branding →. Leave the step without saving and the segments stay as they were.

Once games have started, the segments are locked and shown read-only. Games have started after Start all games or Start round, once a match is called to its board, or once any result is in. From then on the format and the competition type (step 1) of a Teams event can’t change any more either, because every match of the event must be played the same way.

A new Teams event starts with five segments:

  • Foursome 1001, best of 1;
  • Singles 501, best of 3;
  • Doubles 501, best of 3;
  • Singles 501, best of 3;
  • Doubles 501, best of 3.

Change them to suit your league.

The tie-breaker

Under A tie-breaker might be needed you choose where a tie-breaker is used: Use a tie-breaker in round robin matches, Use a tie-breaker in knockout matches, or both. Only the ticks that fit your format are shown. A round robin has the first, a knockout (Group Boards included) the second, and Round robin → Knockout both. When one is ticked, a Tie-breaker row appears at the end with its own type, starting score and best of. It is played only when the two teams have won the same number of legs after the main segments. A new event has both ticked, with a Foursome 1001, best of 1.

Each tick is locked once its own stage has begun, not before. In Round robin → Knockout you can still choose the knockout tie-breaker while the groups are being played. Even after a stage has begun you can switch its tie-breaker on (never off again): the knockout one at any time, and the round robin one as long as no drawn result is saved. The tie-breaker’s type, starting score and best of can change while no stage that has begun uses it.

How a team match is scored, and lineup sheets

#

Enter one result per team match, know the most a team can win and what happens when totals are level, and print lineup sheets.

Entering a team result

You enter one result for the whole match, not one for each segment. Click the match as usual. In each box, type the total legs that team won across all the segments, adding the tie-breaker’s legs if it was played. Then click Save score. Standings, leg difference and the bracket all use those totals.

For example, take the five default segments. One team wins the foursome (1 leg) and one singles 2–1, and loses the other three segments 1–2. It has 1 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 6 legs, and the other team has 0 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 7.

The most a team can win

In each segment a team can win at most the legs it needs to take it: 1 in a best of 1, 2 in a best of 3, 3 in a best of 5. Added up over the segments, that is the most a team can have in one match. With the five default segments it is 9, or 10 in a stage that uses the tie-breaker. The result box says “A team can win at most 10 legs.” It refuses a bigger number with a message: it never changes what you typed. A result saved earlier can always be saved again unchanged.

When the totals are level

  • Where the tie-breaker is used, a level total can’t be saved. Play the tie-breaker and include its legs in the totals.
  • A round robin without the tie-breaker (also the group stage of Round robin → Knockout): a level total is allowed. It is a draw, 1 point each.
  • A knockout without the tie-breaker (Group Boards included): a level total is still refused (“This is a knockout match — it can’t end level.”), because a knockout needs a winner. The message suggests ticking Use a tie-breaker in knockout matches (possible at any time). Or play one more deciding leg and include it in the totals. One more leg always fits: a level total is always below the most a team can win.

A no-show works as in any event: Mark no-show… gives the other team every main segment, which is 9–0 with the defaults.

The lineup sheet

On step 4 or step 7, tick Also print a lineup/scorecard sheet for each fixture, for captains to fill in on the day. A fixture is one team match: one team against another. The pack then gets one page for each fixture whose teams are known, with both teams side by side. Each side has:

  • a dark bar for every segment, such as Singles 1, with its starting score, its legs and a Points box. Singles bars are half the height of the others;
  • numbered lines for the players’ names: one for singles, two for doubles, three for trios, four for a foursome;
  • the TIE-BREAKER bar, when that fixture uses one;
  • a compact TOTAL POINTS box for everything, the tie-breaker included;
  • and, on the rest of the page, a large space for the captain’s signature.

In a knockout, print again as rounds finish to get the next fixtures’ sheets.

The lineup sheet shows the TIE-BREAKER bar only for fixtures of a stage that uses it. The rules & format sheet lists the segments and says where the tie-breaker is used. The time estimate counts every leg of every segment, the tie-breaker’s too in a stage that uses it. Each team match is one fixture on one board: its segments are played one after another and are never scheduled apart.

Knockout lineup sheets as the rounds go on

In the knockout, a match’s lineup sheet is ready as soon as both of its teams are known. That is the first round when the bracket is drawn, and a later match the moment both of its teams have won through. On step 5 (Manage Tournament) the Lineup sheets card lists every match that is ready and not yet played. Click Print lineup on one match, or Print all ready lineup sheets to print every ready sheet you have not printed yet. DartDraw remembers which sheets were printed and marks them Printed. (A sheet counts as printed for the two teams it was printed with.) Print again reprints one. The full-page window of the bracket has the same list behind its Lineup sheets button. Round robin fixtures keep their sheets in the pack.

For a fixture added on the day, or a spoiled sheet, Print empty lineup sheet prints a blank one. It has the event’s name and logo, the segments, the tie-breaker bar as set up, TOTAL POINTS and the signature space. The Group, Board, Fixture, Game No. and team name boxes are left empty. Pick 1 to 10 copies beside the button. It is on the print steps (4 and 7) and in Manage Tournament, and works before anything is drawn.

Words you will see

#

Every word and short label the app shows, in plain words, with an example of where you see it.

Grouped by topic. Each word says what it means, then where you see it, in grey.

Entries and the draw

Entry (player, pair or team)
One side in the event: a player in Singles, a pair in Doubles, a team in Teams. “24 entries” in Doubles means 24 pairs. Step 3: Add entries, Add pair, Add team.
Group
A small set of entries who all play each other (a round robin). Each group prints as one chart. In Group Boards a group is a small knockout instead. “Group A · Board 2” on step 4 and step 5.
Board
A dartboard. Each group plays on its own board or boards; groups that share a board play one after another. Step 2: Number of boards available; step 3: Board(s).
Draw
Who goes in which group, or who meets whom in a bracket. Step 3: Random draw, “Not drawn”, Draw groups.
Seed
A strong entry placed so that the strongest do not meet early. Seeds 1 and 2 can only meet in the final. Step 3: Seed this bracket, Number of seeds.
A1, B2 and other position labels
A letter and a number: the group, then the place in it. “A1” is the winner of Group A, and “A2” its runner-up. Before a draw, an unnamed entry can read “Player A1”. The projected bracket, and the knockout drawn from a round robin.
Bye
A free pass into the next round, because there is no opponent. It happens when the number of entries does not fill the bracket (4, 8, 16, 32, 64 or 128). Step 2: “Bracket size (auto)” with the number of byes; a small BYE tag on printed brackets.
Check-in
A tick for each entry who has arrived. It is only a record for you. Step 4: the Check-in card, Check in all, “10 of 12 checked in”.
Withdraw
Take an entry out before their group starts, with or without a replacement. Step 3: Withdraw.
Confirm
You checked a chart and it is ready to print and play. Step 4: Confirm this chart, “Un-confirmed automatically”.

Matches and results

Leg, and best of
A leg is one game of darts. A match is “best of” a number of legs: in a best of 5 the first to win 3 legs wins. You type the legs each side won, for example 3 and 1. Step 2: Best of legs; printed charts: “Best of 5 Legs”.
Marker
The person who keeps score at the board for a match, usually another entry from the same group who is not playing. DartDraw suggests one for every match; Teams events have none. The match list on each chart: “Match 1 · 1 v 2 · Marker: 3” (the numbers are places in the group).
Start all games, Start round
Start all games opens scoring for every confirmed group at once. Start round in a knockout tells DartDraw when a round began, so it can compare the clock with its estimate. In a Teams event, starting also locks the segments. Step 5.
Walkover (WO)
A win given because the opponent did not turn up. It counts as a played match. A WO tag on the chart or bracket.
No-show
An entry who did not turn up or has left. Marking them gives walkovers to their opponents. Mark no-show… on step 5; a No-show tag on step 3.
Neither showed
Neither side of a knockout match turned up. Nobody goes through from it. The Mark no-show… box; the public page says “Neither attended”.
Match codes: 2-1, P2-1, G3·1-2
Which match in a bracket: the round, then the match in that round. “2-1” is round 2, match 1. A “P” in front is the Plate pool; “G3·” in front is Group 3 of Group Boards. The small tag on each bracket match; split sheets say “to Finals 5-1”.
Round names
Knockout rounds are named from the end: Final, Semifinal, Quarterfinal, and before that “Round of 16”, “Round of 32” and so on (how many places the round has, byes included). Bracket headings; step 2 knockout legs.
Call to a board
In Group Boards, tell the players on the public results page to go to their board now. The Boards card: Call, Uncall, “Called”.

Tables and tie-breaks

Standings
The table under each group chart: who is where, worked out from the results. Step 5: “Standings”; step 6: Finalize Standings.
Pos., MP, MW, ML, MD, LW, +/-, Pts
Position; matches played; matches won; matches lost; matches drawn (shown only for a draw saved in an older event: there are no draws now); legs won; leg difference (legs won minus legs lost); points. A win is 1 point and a loss 0. The standings table; printed charts: “Points: Win 1 · Loss 0”.
TB, H2H, LD, LW and ?
The TB column shows which tie-break separated two entries level on points: H2H (head-to-head: the match between them), LD (leg difference) or LW (legs won). “3-way” means three entries were level. A ? means they are still level after every rule: you decide. The TB column of the standings table.
Tie-break order
The order of those rules. You can change it in Advanced setup. Step 2: Tie-break order; printed charts: “Tie-break: 1) Legs won…”.
* (asterisk)
A position you set by hand. Adjust positions…; printed charts: “* Position set manually by organiser”.
Qualifier, goes through
An entry that finished high enough in its group to go into the knockout. Step 2: Entries per group (advance to knockout); the projected bracket: “12 qualifiers”.

Formats and stages

Round robin
Everyone in a group plays everyone else in that group once. Step 2: Round robin, Round robin → Knockout.
Knockout, bracket
Lose once and you are out. The bracket is the chart of who meets whom, round by round, up to the Champion. Step 2: Knockout, Single bracket; step 5: Knockout stage.
Projected bracket
The knockout as it will look, printed before the groups are played, with position labels like A1 instead of names. The print pack: “Main bracket (projected)”.
Plate pool
A second knockout for the entries who did not go through from the groups, so they keep playing. It has its own winner. Step 2 (Advanced setup): Plate pool; match codes starting with P.
Group Boards, Finals
A knockout for a big field: small knockout groups, each on its own board, then the group champions meet in a Finals bracket. Step 2: Group Boards, then finals; “Winner of Group 3” in the Finals.

Teams

Segment
One part of a team match, for example “Singles 1” or a foursome. The team’s result is the total legs from all the segments. Step 2: Team match segments (played in order).
Singles, Doubles, Trios, Foursome
A segment for one, two, three or four players from each team. The segment type on step 2.
Tie-breaker (Teams)
An extra segment, played only when the team totals are level. Step 2: A tie-breaker might be needed; the lineup sheet: TIE-BREAKER.
Fixture
One team match: one team against another, on one board. The Fixture box on the lineup sheet.
Lineup sheet
A page for each fixture where the captains write who plays each segment, and the points. Step 4 or 7: Also print a lineup/scorecard sheet for each fixture, for captains to fill in on the day.

Printing, sharing and finishing

Blank pack, running order, rules sheet
What you print before play: the charts with no scores, a master running order (board by board, every match with a tick box) and a rules & format sheet. Step 4: Print bracket pack.
Printed sheets language
The language every printed sheet of the event uses, English or Bahasa Malaysia, whatever language your screen shows. See Printed sheets language. Steps 4 and 7: Printed sheets language.
EN | BM
The language switch: the words on your screen in English or Bahasa Malaysia. It changes only the words, never your event. See The EN | BM switch. The top bar, next to Help.
Publish, list
Publish puts the results on a public page that anyone with the link can see. List also shows the event on the public home page. Step 5: Publish results, List on the public page.
QR code
A square code printed on charts and bracket sheets. Phones scan it to open the live results. It only prints while the event is published. Printed sheets: “Live results · Scan, or go to” and the address.
Full-page window
Manage Tournament in its own window, for a second screen or projector. Step 5: Open in new window ↗.
Finalize
Accept the results as final. The public page then says Final results, and only an Admin can reopen the event. The bottom of step 5: Accept & finalize.
Incident log
Your private notes about warnings or disputes. Never printed or shown. Step 5: + Log incident.
Sample event
A practice event that is never saved unless you click Save as my event. The welcome screen: Try a sample event.

Frequently asked questions

Can I run two events at once?

Yes. Each event is separate. Click + New tournament for the second one, or open it from Saved tournaments. Open the app in a second browser tab if you want to look at both together. Just do not open the same event in two places and score in both. A score entered in a window that is out of date is not saved. If you see “Updated elsewhere”, click Reload before you enter anything else, then enter your last change again.

Can I use DartDraw offline?

No. Your events are saved to your account on the internet, so you need a connection to sign in and to save. If the connection drops, DartDraw shows Couldn’t save — click to retry. Keep the page open, get back online and click the message, and wait for Saved before you open another event (DartDraw keeps you on the event until then). It is a good idea to print the blank pack before play in case the venue wifi is poor.

How many entries can I have?

There is no fixed limit for a round robin. A knockout bracket holds up to 128 slots. For big fields, use Group Boards, which allows up to 128 groups of 2 to 128 players each. If you see a message that your account allows up to a certain number of entries per event, contact your admin.

How do I change a score after the event is finalized?

You cannot. Only an Admin can reopen a finalized event, so ask your admin. Once it is reopened, reload the event, fix the score and accept the results again. Before you finalize, you can correct any score by clicking it and saving a new result. See Finishing the event and Correcting or clearing a result.

How do I correct or clear a result?

Click the match. To correct it, type the right legs and click Save score. To empty it, click Clear result, then Clear result again in the box that asks “Clear this result?”. The match goes back to not played. In a knockout, a new winner (or a cleared match) also clears every later match the old winner had reached, and the champion. When any of those already has a result, DartDraw lists them first and you choose Change result or Cancel. A new score with the same winner changes nothing later. Once the knockout has been drawn from a round robin, group results can be corrected but not cleared. See Correcting or clearing a result.

Can spectators see the names of players in a pair?

They see the pair name you type, and the organisation if you gave one. There are no separate boxes for the two players, so if you want both names to show, put both in the pair name, such as both surnames together. A team is shown by its team name in the same way.

Why is my event not on the public home page?

Go through this list:

  1. Is Publish results ticked on the Public results card (bottom of step 5)?
  2. Is List on the public page ticked? It is off by default, and unticking Publish results turns it off.
  3. Wait about half a minute and refresh the home page.
  4. Look at the right tab. Live needs play to have started and the event not finalized. Upcoming is for events that have not started and have a date of yesterday or later. Results is for finalized events, and only for a limited time.
  5. If the card says the address of the public site is not set up, tell your admin.

A duplicated event starts unpublished and unlisted, so tick both again.

How do tie-breaks work?

Entries are ranked by points first. A win is 1 point and a loss 0. There are no draws: a score must have a winner, and in a Teams round robin a level total goes to the tie-breaker.

When entries have the same points, DartDraw uses your Tie-break order. The default is:

  1. Legs won: the most legs won over the whole group.
  2. Leg difference: legs won minus legs lost over the whole group.
  3. Head-to-head result: whoever won the match between them goes ahead.
  4. Organiser decision / playoff: nothing automatic. It is down to you.

With three or more entries level, head-to-head, leg difference and legs won use only the matches those entries played against each other. If a rule separates some of them, the entries still level are sorted again by the rules, in order.

If every rule leaves two entries level, a ? badge appears and a warning says they need an organiser decision or a playoff. Settle it, then set the order with Adjust positions…. Steps 6 and 7, and drawing the knockout when the tie is on the cut-off line, stay locked until you do. In Advanced setup you can reorder the rules under Rules on step 2.

What does “best of 5” mean when I enter a score?

The first to win most of the legs wins the match. In a best of 5 that is 3 legs. The score box accepts only finished results: the winner must have exactly 3, and the loser 0, 1 or 2. In a best of 3 the winner needs 2, and so on.

How do I enter the result of a team match?

As one result: the total legs each team won across all the segments, including the tie-breaker if it was played. A team cannot have more than the most it could win (9 with the default segments, 10 where the tie-breaker is used). Where the tie-breaker is used, the totals can’t be level. Without it, level totals are a draw in a round robin, and are refused in a knockout. See Entering a team result.

How are byes decided in a knockout?

The bracket size is the next one up from your entries (4, 8, 16, 32, 64 or 128). The empty places are byes in round 1. They go to the best seeds. With seeding off, the draw is random. Entries from the same organisation are kept apart as long as possible whenever an organisation is filled in. After a round robin, entries from the same group come first, then the same organisation. Each draw takes about a second at most. In a big bracket with many entries from one organisation, the spread is the best DartDraw finds in that time, which can be slightly less even than possible. A bracket holds at most 128 entries: DartDraw refuses a bigger one and says which setting to change.

What is the difference between Basic and Advanced setup?

Basic setup shows only the essentials. Advanced setup adds scheduling (level of play, start time, breaks), different best-of-legs for later knockout rounds, the Plate pool, sponsor logos with a footer line, and your own tie-break order. You can switch whenever you like and nothing you set is lost. See Basic or Advanced setup.

What is a Plate pool?

In Round robin → Knockout, entries that do not advance can play their own knockout, called the Plate pool, so they still get matches. Turn it on in Advanced setup on step 2. You can limit how many enter with Plate entries per group. See Round robin → Knockout.

What is Group Boards, and do I have to use it?

No, it is optional. It is a kind of Knockout: choose Knockout, then Group Boards, then finals. It splits a big field into small knockout groups. Each group has its own board and ends with a champion, and the champions play a Finals bracket. It suits large events. For a normal-sized field, a round robin or a knockout is usually simpler. See Group Boards.

Can I change the format after I have started?

Change the format before you start the games. Go back to step 2, make the change and save. Round robin groups adjust to fit. Group Boards cannot be redrawn once scores are in. Once scores are entered it is safest to leave the format alone.

Why can’t I change the entries, swap or redraw after the games start?

Because results already played belong to the entries who played them. Once a group has started (after Start all games), has a result, or the knockout has been drawn, DartDraw refuses anything that would move or remove those entries, and says why: Withdraw, Swap with… and Random draw on step 3. Entries can be removed only before the draw; after it, they are withdrawn. Before the games start all of these work as usual. To take someone out once play has begun, use Mark no-show…. See A player leaves part-way.

Can I move a player to another group?

Yes, on step 3. In a round robin use Swap with…. In Group Boards use Move… before you confirm the groups. A confirmed group whose entries change is un-confirmed for you. Do this before games start: once a group has started, DartDraw refuses to swap or move its entries. See Why can’t I change the entries after the games start?

Someone did not turn up. What do I do?

Once play has started, use Mark no-show…. In a round robin it fills their unplayed matches with walkover losses. In a knockout it gives the opponent a walkover win. If both are missing in a knockout or Group Boards match, choose Neither showed (a round robin does not have that choice). Before their group starts, in a round robin you can Withdraw them; once it has started, Withdraw is refused and offers Mark no-show instead. See No-shows and walkovers and A player leaves part-way.

Who is the Admin, and how do I reach them?

The Admin is the person who runs your DartDraw site. They create organiser accounts, can send a password reset link, and are the only person who can reopen a finalized event. Contact them the way you normally would. DartDraw has no built-in way to message them.

I forgot my password

Ask your admin for a reset link. You can also click Forgot password? on the sign-in form and type your email; if a link arrives, follow it, and if nothing arrives, ask your admin. Using a reset link signs you out on every device.

Can I print on A3 or bigger?

Knockout brackets can be printed on A4, A3, A2 or A1. Pick the Knockout bracket paper size on the print screen; the fit check under it shows how big the names print on each size. If a bracket is too big for the size you chose, DartDraw asks whether to use the size it recommends, split the bracket over A4 sheets, or keep your size and shrink it. Round robin charts and other sheets are A4 landscape. See Knockout bracket sheets and paper size.

How quickly do spectators see new scores?

Their page refreshes itself about every 20 seconds, and picks up scores shortly after you save them. The home page list can take about half a minute to catch up.

Can I reuse an event for next time?

Yes. Click Duplicate under the event in Saved tournaments. You get a copy with “(copy)” added to the name. It is open for editing, not published and not listed. The copy keeps the entries, settings and any scores, so change what you need.

Is the incident log public?

No. It is only for you. It is never printed and never appears on the public results page.

Can I use the same account on two devices?

Yes. Sign in on both. Your events are the same on each. Just score each event on one device at a time. A score entered on a device that is out of date is not saved. If you see “Updated elsewhere — click to reload”, click Reload on the red banner before you enter anything else, then check your latest scores and enter your last change again.

Why did a chart say “Un-confirmed automatically”?

You changed who is in that group after confirming it, for example by swapping or withdrawing an entry. DartDraw un-confirms it so you check it again. Click Confirm this chart once it is right.

Does the full-page window update by itself?

Not while you are looking at it. Each window loads the saved event when it opens. When you switch to a window, it checks for a newer copy and loads it, with the note “Updated with the latest scores” (not on steps 1 to 3, not while one of your changes is still saving or a box is open, and not while you have typed something not yet saved). Windows in the same browser tell each other when they save, so this works straight away; for a change made on another device it checks at most every few seconds. An out-of-date window cannot write over newer scores: its save is refused and it shows “Updated elsewhere — reload to get the latest”, but the score you entered there is not saved. Press F5 to bring a window up to date, and score in one window at a time. See Keeping the windows in step.

Can I use my phone?

Yes, held sideways (landscape), but DartDraw works best on a tablet, laptop or desktop. Held upright, a phone or tablet shows “Please rotate your device to landscape to use DartDraw.” Turn it and the app is back as you left it. On a phone on its side, a slim note says DartDraw works best on a bigger screen (tap × to hide it). The left-hand menu becomes a bar at the top: tap the step name to change step, open another event, rename, duplicate or delete this one, or change your password. Setting up an event is easier on a bigger screen; a phone is fine for checking and entering scores.

Can I delete or rename an event?

Yes, with Rename and Delete under the event in Saved tournaments. Deleting removes it from your account for good. You cannot rename or delete a finalized event. Ask your admin.
