Following a player

When and where does my player play?

It depends on the kind of event. (In a Teams event, read “team” for “player”.)

  • Group Boards events have a Boards tab. It shows each board, the match on it now, the next few, and a rough time. A player’s page also says things like “Board 2 · 1 match ahead · ~7:50 pm”. A time with a ~ is an estimate and can run late.
  • Round robin groups show the group on a player’s page, its board if the organiser gave one, and who they still have to play. They do not show the order or a time.
  • Knockout events show a match as soon as both sides are known, for example “Next: Semifinal (3-1) vs Leo Ahmad” (the code in brackets is the match code), or “opponent not decided yet” while one side is still to be decided. They do not show a board or a time.

In Round robin and Knockout events the organiser calls each match at the venue, so this site can’t say where or when. Listen for the announcements, or ask at the organiser’s desk.

How do I find a player?

Type part of a name in the Find a player box under the tabs. Matching names appear below the box. Tap one, or press Enter to open the first.

You can also tap any name in a table or a bracket.

A player’s page shows their position, points and leg difference, who they have played, and who is next.

Before the draw is made, the event page lists nobody yet, so there is no search box. Names appear once the organiser makes the draw.

I can’t find my name in the search

Try a shorter part of your name, or your club if you know it. Search matches part of a name, so you don’t need to spell it all out. If you still can’t find it, you may not be in the draw yet, or the organiser may have spelled it differently.

What are Me and Following?

On a player’s page, tap This is me to make that player you. The Me tab then opens straight to your group standing and next match each time you come back. Me is one player.

Tap Follow to keep an eye on someone else. Follow as many as you like. They all appear together on the Following tab, which shows how many you follow.

You are marked in blue and the players you follow are marked with a star and gold.

How do I come back later?

Open the same link again, or scan the QR code again. If you set Me or followed someone, the page opens on your Me or Following tab, unless the link itself names a screen (some QR codes open one group).

Me and Following are kept on this device, for each event. The home page lists the events the organiser chose to show there.

What does the line under a player’s name say?

It says where that player is right now. For example who they play next, how many matches they have left in their group, their place when the group is finished, or that they are out and who they lost to.

A player who left the event is shown as Withdrawn.

Is this up to date?

How often does the page refresh?

An event page checks for new scores about every 20 seconds while it is open. You don’t need to reload it. The home page checks about every 30 seconds.

There can be a short delay between the organiser entering a score and you seeing it. The page checking often does not mean the scores change often. Look at Scores last changed to see when they last did.

The scores aren’t moving
  1. Look at Scores last changed next to the pill at the top. That is when the organiser last saved a change. If it is recent, you have the latest scores, and the next result just hasn’t been entered yet.
  2. Results appear when the organiser types them in, often a minute or two after a match ends. A match may still be in play.
  3. If the time is old and you know matches have finished, reload the page, or open it again from the home page.
  4. If the pill says Offline, see It says Offline.

If it still looks wrong, ask the organiser.

What do “Starting soon”, “Live” and “Final results” mean?
Starting soon
The draw is out but no match has been played yet.
Live
Matches are being played and scores are coming in.
Final results
The organiser has marked the event as finished. Nothing more will change.
Offline
Your phone hasn’t been able to reach the site for a while. The page keeps trying, and the scores on screen stay as they were.

Next to the pill you will see Scores last changed and a time. That is when the organiser last saved a change to the event, usually a score. It is not when your phone last checked, so it can be a while ago when no match has finished. If it is not today, the date is shown too.

When does an event move to Results?

Only after the organiser marks the event Final results. Until then it stays in Live while the organiser is still updating it, even if the last match has been played, and its card may say “Awaiting final results”. If it goes quiet for a while without being marked final, it drops off the home page until it is. The link you were given keeps working.

Results stay on the home page for a limited time, then they are removed. If you want to keep a result, note it down or take a screenshot.

It says “Offline”, “Reconnecting” or “Couldn’t load results”

Your phone can’t reach the site right now, maybe because of weak signal or venue Wi-Fi. Keep the page open. It keeps trying and will fill in on its own when the connection comes back.

While it can’t refresh, the scores on screen stay as they were. Scores last changed tells you how old they are. Last checked is when your phone last reached the site.

Try switching between Wi-Fi and mobile data.

It says “Results not available”

The link isn’t valid, or the organiser has taken the results offline. Check you copied the whole link. If an organiser republishes the event, the page comes back on its own, so it is worth keeping the tab open. Otherwise tap See all events to go to the home page.

Reading the results

What are the tabs at the top?

Which tabs you see depends on the event.

Me
The one player you picked as yourself.
Following
Any players you chose to follow.
Groups
A table and a results grid for each group.
Boards
Which match is on which board. Only in Group Boards events.
Bracket
The knockout draw, round by round.

The page opens on the part of the event being played now: for example Bracket once the knockout has been drawn, or Boards while the groups of a Group Boards event are playing. If you set Me or follow someone, it opens on Me or Following instead. Tap any tab to switch; the page won’t move you while you are reading.

Before the draw is made, the page says so and asks you to check back soon.

Group tables

What do MP, MW, ML, LW, +/-, Pts and TB mean?
MP
Matches played.
MW
Matches won.
ML
Matches lost.
MD
Matches drawn. This column only appears if a match in the group was drawn.
LW
Legs won, across all of the player’s matches.
+/-
Leg difference: legs won minus legs lost.
Pts
Points. See How do points work?
TB
Tie-break badge. Only appears when players are level on points.

The first column is the position in the group.

Under each group table, tap What do the columns mean? for a short key, on a phone too.

How do points work?

Win a match: 2 points. Draw: 1 point. Lose: 0 points. Players are ranked by points first.

Who goes through to the knockout?

In events with groups and then a knockout, the top places in each group go through. The line above the group tables says how many, for example “Top 2 in each group go through to the knockout”.

A green bar beside the position marks those places, in the tables on the Groups tab and in a player’s group summary. Until a group has played all its matches, the places can still change.

If the event also has a Plate pool, the same line says who plays in it. In a group with no more players than that number, everyone goes through.

What happens when players are level on points?

Ties are split by rules, applied one at a time until the players are separated. The usual order is:

  1. Head-to-head: the result of the match between them.
  2. Leg difference: legs won minus legs lost.
  3. Legs won.

The organiser sets the order for each event, so it can differ. If every rule leaves players level, the organiser decides, or there is a playoff.

While a group is still being played, tie notes are marked Provisional because they can change as matches finish.

What if three or more players are level?

Then only the matches between those players are counted. A small mini-table under the group shows exactly what was compared. This matters because results can go in a circle: A beat B, B beat C, and C beat A.

The same rules are used inside the mini-table: head-to-head (its points), then leg difference, then legs won.

What do the TB badges mean?

A badge shows which rule put a player ahead of the player beside them when they were level on points.

H2H
Head-to-head. They won the match against each other.
LD
Leg difference.
LW
Legs won.
3-way LD
Three players were level, so the mini-table of their matches against each other was used, and leg difference split them. 3-way H2H and 3-way LW work the same way, and the number can be larger.
?
Still level after every rule. It needs an organiser decision or a playoff.

An asterisk (*) after a position means the organiser set that position by hand.

On a computer, hover over a badge for a short explanation. On a phone, tap What do the columns mean? under the table.

How do I read the results grid?

Under each group table is a grid of every match. Find your row, then read across to the opponent’s column. The score shows the row player’s legs first.

3–1
Green: the row player won.
1–3
Red: the row player lost.
2–2
Bold with no colour: a draw.
·
Not played yet.
  
Shaded square: a player can’t play themselves.

A small WO next to a score means a walkover: one player didn’t turn up. On a phone the grid scrolls sideways if it is wider than the screen.

Brackets

What are the Rounds and Chart views?

Rounds shows one round at a time as big match cards. Use the round buttons (Quarters, Semis and so on) to move between them. A tick on a button means that round is finished. This is the default on phones.

Chart shows the whole draw as a bracket diagram. It is the default on wide screens. Tap Fit to screen to shrink it to the screen width, and Full size to go back.

Switch any time with the Rounds / Chart buttons. The page remembers your choice on this device. Large draws (32 or more) open in Rounds unless you have chosen Chart.

What do To play, Waiting, Bye and the other labels mean?
To play
Both players are known and the match hasn’t been played.
Waiting
At least one player isn’t decided yet. It will fill in as earlier matches finish. A slot may say “Winner of” and the match it comes from.
Bye
The player goes straight to the next round without playing.
Walkover
One player didn’t turn up, so the other went through. The one who didn’t is marked “did not show”.
Neither attended
Neither player turned up, so nobody went through that match.
Not played
The match didn’t happen and no player was ever decided for it.

In the Chart view a match nobody attended shows NO-SHOW beside both names, and a bye shows BYE.

A small WO next to a player in a bracket means a walkover. That player didn’t turn up, so the other player went through. On a player’s page the result is written in full: “Won 2–0 by walkover” for the one who went through, “Lost 0–2 by walkover” for the one who didn’t turn up.

A number or a label like A1 before a name is a seed or group place. Under each bracket, tap What do the labels mean? for a short key.

How do I see who won?

In a finished match the winner is shown in bold with a green score, and the loser is greyed out. When the final is played, a Winner banner appears at the top of the bracket, with the runner-up under it.

The Final results pill in the page header means the organiser has signed off the whole event.

What are the stage cards and the progress line?

Some events have two stages, for example groups first and then a knockout, or groups and then the Finals. Two cards near the top show where each stage is: not started, in progress with the number of matches played, or complete. Tap a card to jump to that stage.

In the header, a line such as “2 of 4 groups finished” shows how far the group stage has got. Under the title you may also see how many legs a match is, for example “Best of 5 legs” (see Best of). In a Teams event it says how many segments a match has instead (see Teams).

Kinds of event

Group Boards events

What is a Group Boards event?

Players are split into small groups, for example Group 3. Each group plays its own knockout on its own board or boards. The winner of each group goes through to the Finals.

A player’s page shows where they are drawn, like “Group 3 · Board 3”.

What is on the Boards tab?

One card per board. It shows the match that is on now, then the next few. You may see:

  • Up now: this match is next to be played on that board.
  • Called — go to Board N: the players have been called to that board.
  • Waiting for an earlier match: it can’t start until another match finishes.
  • A time like “~7:10 pm”: an estimate, not a promise.

If you set yourself as Me, your page also shows your next board and how many matches are ahead of yours.

How do I switch between Finals and the groups?

On the Bracket tab, use the picker at the top. Choose Finals, All groups, or one group. Groups still playing are listed apart from the completed ones. If you are set as Me, a My group button takes you straight to your group.

While the groups are playing, the Bracket tab opens on All groups (or on your own group, if you set Me and your group is still playing). All groups shows each group as a card with its boards, the number of players, and its winner once it is finished. Tap a card to open that group. Once every group is finished it opens on the Finals.

Doubles

How are doubles shown?

In a doubles event each entry is a pair. It is shown by the pair name the organiser gave it, which is often both surnames. Long pair names may wrap onto a second line. The page doesn’t list the two people separately.

The pages still say “player”. Everything works the same: tap a pair to open its page, follow it, or mark it as Me. The search box matches the pair name or the club, so type what is shown for the pair.

Teams

How does a team match work?

A team match is played in segments, one after another and in order, for example a foursome, then some singles and some doubles. Each segment is a best of a set number of legs, and the organiser chooses how many segments a match has.

The teams’ legs from every segment are added up. The team with more legs in total wins the match.

If the totals are level, a tie-breaker segment is played, but only in the stages where the organiser chose to use one. Where there is none, a level result is a draw in a round robin, and a knockout match is not allowed to end level.

Why is a result a total like 7–3?

The page shows one result for the whole match: the total legs each team won across all its segments, with the tie-breaker’s legs included if it was played. So 7–3 means one team won seven legs in total and the other won three.

The tables, grids and brackets use those totals the same way they use legs in other events, so LW and +/- add up the totals. The page doesn’t break a result down by segment.

How do I find a team?

In a Teams event each entry is a team, shown by its team name. Type part of the team name, or its club, in the Find a team box under the tabs, then tap the team to open its page.

You can follow a team or mark it as Me, just like a player in other events. The page doesn’t list the people in a team.

What does “5 segments + tie-breaker per match” under the title mean?

In a Teams event this line takes the place of “Best of 5 legs”. It says how many segments each match has, and adds + tie-breaker when that stage uses a tie-breaker (it is only played if the totals are level).

If the round robin and the knockout differ, each is shown on its own, for example “Round robin: 5 segments per match · Knockout: 5 segments + tie-breaker per match”. In Group Boards events the groups and the Finals use the knockout setting.

Plate pool

What is the Plate pool?

Some events with groups and then a knockout also run a Plate pool: a second knockout for the players who didn’t go through to the main bracket. The line above the group tables says who plays in it.

Its bracket is on the Bracket tab, under the main bracket, and it has its own winner. Its match codes start with P, for example P1-2.

Finding events

What do Live, Upcoming and Results mean?

Live events have started: at least one match has been played, and the scores are coming in.

Upcoming events are listed but haven’t started yet. They show the date and, if the organiser gave one, the start time.

Results are finished events. Tap one to see the winner and every score.

The small number on each tab is how many events are in it. Tap any event to open it.

How do I search for an event?

Type in the search box on the home page. It looks at the event name, the tournament name, the format and the winner’s name. It only searches the tab you are on.

You can also paste an event link or code there to open it directly.

Why can’t I see an event?

The home page only lists events the organiser chose to show there. Some organisers share a link or a code instead.

If you have a link or a code, paste it into the search box at the top. An Open event button appears. Tap it.

Other reasons an event may be missing:

  • The organiser hasn’t published it yet.
  • It was taken offline.
  • It stopped being updated a while ago, so it dropped off Live.
  • It finished, but the organiser hasn’t marked it as final yet, and it has gone quiet (see When does an event move to Results?).

Not sure? Ask the organiser for the event link.

The home page says it can’t load events

The page couldn’t reach the site the first time, so it has no list to show yet. It does not mean nothing is on. The numbers on the tabs only appear once the list has loaded.

Check your connection, for example weak signal or venue Wi-Fi, then tap Try again. The page also keeps trying by itself, so you can leave it open.

If you have an event link, you can open it directly instead.

Links and QR codes

What are the QR codes on the organiser’s sheets?

The organiser may print sheets with QR codes. Point your phone camera at one and open the link it shows. It takes you to the live page for that event, so you don’t need to type anything. Some codes open a particular group or the Finals bracket.

You can also type or paste the code that goes with the event into the home page search box.

Can I share a link to what I’m looking at?

Yes. Copy the address from your browser and send it. The part after the # says which screen to open, such as one group, the bracket, or one player’s page, so the other person lands in the same place.

Your own Me and Following choices are not part of the link. They stay on your device.

Something wrong or missing

I think a score is wrong. Who do I tell?

The scores are typed in by the organiser at the venue. This site only shows them, and it has no way to send the organiser a message.

Tell the organiser or the scorer’s desk. Once they fix it, the page shows the change by itself at its next refresh.

The page is slow or the table looks cut off

On a slow connection the page can take a moment to load.

Wide tables and bracket charts scroll sideways. Swipe along them. For brackets, Rounds is easier on a small screen.

It says “We can’t find that player”

The player may have been taken out of the draw, or the link is old. Go back and search for the name again. (In a Teams event the page says “team”.) If you set that player as Me and they were removed, the Me tab asks you to pick again.

Words you will see

Words you will see: a short glossary

Plain meanings for the words and short codes on the event pages, with an example of where you see each one.

Leg

One game of darts. A match is a set number of legs, and the score of a match is the legs each side won, for example 3–1.

Best of

“Best of 5 legs” means the match is up to 5 legs: the first to win 3 wins it. The line under the event name says how many, for example “Knockout: best of 3 legs, final best of 5”.

Draw

How the organiser places the players into groups or a bracket. Names appear on the page once the draw is made. (A drawn match, level on legs, is something else: see MD under the columns.)

Stage

One part of an event, for example the groups and then the knockout. The stage cards near the top show each one.

Seed

A number before a name in the first round of a bracket, like “1 Aiden Tan”. It is the organiser’s ranking before the event: 1 is the top seed.

A1, B2 (group places)

In a knockout after groups, the label before a name says where that player finished in their group: A1 won Group A, B2 was second in Group B.

Match codes: 1-2, G2·1-1, P2-1

Each bracket match has a code: 1-2 is round 1, match 2. In a Group Boards event, G2·1-1 is Group 2’s bracket, round 1, match 1. In the Plate pool the code starts with P, like P2-1. A player’s page uses it too: “Next: Semifinal (3-1)”.

Bye

No opponent in that round, so the player goes straight through. Shown as Bye in Rounds and BYE in the Chart.

Walkover (WO)

A match won because the opponent didn’t turn up. In a bracket chart a small WO marks the player who didn’t turn up, and in a results grid it follows the score. On a player’s page it is written in full: “Won 2–0 by walkover” or “Lost 0–2 by walkover”.

No-show, Neither attended

Neither player turned up, so nobody goes through from that match. Rounds says Neither attended; the Chart shows NO-SHOW beside both names.

Board

The dartboard a match is played on, numbered at the venue, for example “Board 2”. A group may show its board; Group Boards events show each match’s board on the Boards tab.

MP, MW, ML, MD, LW, +/-, Pts

Matches played, won, lost and drawn; legs won; leg difference (legs won minus legs lost); points (2 for a win, 1 for a draw). See the columns.

TB, tie-break, tie-breaker

TB in a group table is the tie-break badge: which rule put a player ahead of one level on points. A tie-breaker in a Teams event is different: an extra segment played when the team totals are level.

H2H

Head-to-head: the result of the match between the two players who are level on points.

3-way LD

Three players were level on points, so only the matches between those three were counted (a mini-table), and leg difference in that mini-table split them. “3-way H2H” and “3-way LW” work the same way.

Qualify, go through

Finish high enough in a group to play in the knockout. The line above the group tables says how many, for example “Top 2 in each group go through to the knockout”, and a green bar marks those places.

Plate pool

A second knockout for the players who didn’t go through to the main bracket, with its own winner. See What is the Plate pool?

Group Boards

A kind of event: players are split into small groups, each plays its own knockout on its own board, and each group winner goes to the Finals.

Final results

The organiser has checked and finished the event, so the scores won’t change any more. The pill under the event name says Final results.

Segment (Teams)

One part of a team match, such as one singles or doubles game. The legs from every segment are added up for the team’s result, for example 7–3.

Pair (Doubles)

Two players who play together as one entry, shown by one pair name like “Tan / Lim”. The pages still call a pair a “player”.

Settings and privacy

How do I switch between light and dark?

On an event page and on this page, use the Auto / Light / Dark switch at the top right. Auto follows your phone’s setting.

On the home page, tap the round button at the top right. Each tap moves through Auto, Light and Dark.

Your choice is remembered on this device, for every page on this site.

Do I need an account?

No. You can look at any event without signing up or giving your name.

Where are Me and Following saved?

Only on your own device, in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere. That also means they don’t follow you to another phone or browser, and clearing your browser’s site data removes them. Each event keeps its own Me and Following.

The “New here?” tips are remembered the same way once you tap Got it on the home page, or the × on an event page.

What does the page show about players?

Only what the organiser chose to publish for that event: the draw, names, scores and standings. This page can’t change anything.
